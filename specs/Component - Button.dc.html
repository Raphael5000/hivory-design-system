Guidelines

The discipline.

Do
One primary per view. Reserve green for the single hero CTA. Name the consequence in the label. Hold width during loading so nothing jumps.
Don't
Two green buttons in one view. Green as a generic accent. Vague labels (OK, Submit). A row of filled buttons where a split or a ghost belongs.

Seventeen tokens, the widest set in the system — five fills, two on-fill inks, a focus ring and the pill radius. That breadth is why every other action in the portal should mount Button rather than restyle a native one.
Variants
primary (ink fill — the default) · brand (--action-brand, black label, ONE per view, hero CTA only) · outline · ghost · danger. Sizes sm 26 / md 33 / lg 40, matching the portal’s real ladder.
The eight states
Every interactive component provides all eight or declares which do not apply, with the reason. A silent omission is the bug this table exists to prevent.
Rest Pill at --radius-pill, weight 600, its variant fill. Heights 26 · 33 · 40.
Hover The --action-*-hover fill; outline deepens its ring to 26% ink. Eases at --duration-soft — hover settles, it never snaps.
Pressed brightness(0.94) from motion-base. No scale, no translate, no shadow change — nothing lifts in this system.
Focus 2px --border-focus outline at 2px offset, :focus-visible only, so a mouse click never draws a ring but Tab always does.
Selected n/a a button acts and returns. Persistent selection belongs to Tabs or a nav item.
Disabled opacity .45 with cursor not-allowed, and the brightness press is suppressed. Never hidden — a disabled button still teaches what is possible.
Loading The label holds its width and the dual-hex Spinner takes the glyph slot. Never swap the label to “Loading…”: the width jump is worse than the wait.
Empty n/a a button with no label is an IconButton, which is a different component with a required tooltip.
Content rules
Verb first, and the verb names the real consequence: “Approve & publish”, never “Submit”, “Confirm” or “OK”. Sentence case, two to four words, no trailing punctuation. A destructive verb says what is destroyed (“Delete template”, not “Delete”). Never “Click here”. On a split button the main segment carries the default consequence and the chevron carries alternatives — the label never describes the chevron.
