Principles

Two rules.

One surface level
Cards sit on the canvas — never a card inside a card. Group with hairlines and inset wells instead.
Elevation = priority
Exactly one elevated card per screen — the thing needing the user. Everything else rests on the hairline + whisper shadow.
Guidelines

The discipline.

Do
Solid cards on the canvas. One glass object (the rail) and one elevated card per screen. Divide with hairlines. Let the glass float over real content so its depth reads.
Don't
Cards nested in cards. Glass on more than one object, or over a blank background (it just looks murky). Heavy drop shadows. Two elevated cards competing.

Six tokens is correct for a surface primitive. It carries no motion at all: a card never animates, only what sits inside it does.
Variants
Card (padded body) · Card flush (for tables, body padding removed) · Stat (metric tile with mono value and pinned delta). Elevation is not a variant — it is a per-screen budget of one.
The eight states
Every interactive component provides all eight or declares which do not apply, with the reason. A silent omission is the bug this table exists to prevent.
Rest --surface-card at --radius-xl with --shadow-card: a whisper shadow and a 0.5px ring. Exactly one card per screen may use --shadow-elevated.
Hover n/a a card is a surface, not a control. When the whole card is clickable the wash goes on its inner row and the card itself never moves.
Pressed n/a press feedback belongs to the control inside.
Focus n/a focus lands on the controls it contains, never the container.
Selected A 2px --ink-900 ring replaces the hairline. Never a green fill and never a shadow change — elevation means priority here, not selection.
Disabled n/a a surface has no disabled state; its contents do.
Loading The header renders for real and the body becomes Skeleton lines, so the card does not change size when content lands. Never a spinner centred in a card.
Empty One sentence naming what will appear, plus the single action that fills it. Never an illustration and never the words “No data”.
Content rules
Title is a two-to-four word noun phrase in sentence case — “Traffic sources”, never “Here are your traffic sources”. Subtitle is one clause and only when the title genuinely needs it. One action in the header, top-right; a second action means the card is doing two jobs.
