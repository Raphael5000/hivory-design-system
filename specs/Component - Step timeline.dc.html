Design System
Component · Step timeline

A machine reporting on itself.

Three or four named phases, one of them happening now. It is the visible half of a job the client has already approved — so it offers nothing to click, and its only duty is to be honest about where the work has got to.

Anatomy

Four parts, in this order.

The mark, turningThe dual-hex spinner at --duration-spinner in six-step gear clicks — the brand's own activity primitive, never a generic ring. It says something is happening before any word is read.
Phase label15px/650, present participle, and it MORPHS as phases pass — Building → Deploying to staging → Verifying. This line carries the meaning and it is the only part announced to a screen reader.
ElapsedTabular figures at --ink-600, counting up. Never a percentage and never an estimate: a made-up ETA that slips is worse than an honest clock.
The rail78px per phase, 16px nodes, a 2px connector between. Done = green fill + white check. Active = green fill + 5px white core + radiating nodePulse. Upcoming = white with a 1.5px ink ring. Connectors tint green as phases pass. Labels warm to ink and 650 as they are reached.
The rail is
decorativearia-hidden, always. It is a PICTURE of the label above it — announcing both would read the same information twice, once as prose and once as a list of nodes. The label carries aria-live="polite" so each phase change is spoken exactly once.
The distinction

Not a Stepper.

They look alike and behave oppositely. Choosing wrongly gives a client a control that does nothing, or takes away one they needed.

Who advances itStepper: the person. Step timeline: the machine. Nothing the client does moves this rail, which is why no part of it is clickable or takes a tab stop.
Can you go backStepper: yes — everything behind you is yours. Step timeline: no. A build does not un-deploy, so a completed phase is history, not a destination.
What it ends inStepper: a decision. Step timeline: an arrival — the hex-check, "Live on staging", the URL as a pill. The timeline is replaced by its own outcome, and does not linger at 3 of 3.
Where it livesStepper: the proposal's rail, permanently. Step timeline: inside an artifact body for the duration of a job, then gone. It is a state the panel passes through, never furniture.

The eight states

Most do not apply, and that is the point.

RestThere is no rest. A step timeline exists only while a job runs; if it is on screen, something is happening. An idle rail means the caller forgot to unmount it.
HoverDoes not apply. Nothing here responds to a pointer, so nothing may hint that it would.
PressedDoes not apply. No control, no press. The only button in the vicinity is the artifact footer's, and it belongs to the panel.
FocusDoes not apply — deliberately. The rail takes no tab stop, because a focus stop that cannot be actioned promises an affordance that is not there. The live label is how the keyboard user receives it.
SelectedDoes not apply. The active phase is not a selection — nobody chose it. It is where the machine happens to be.
DisabledDoes not apply. Upcoming phases look quiet but are not disabled: they are facts about the future, not controls withheld.
LoadingLoading IS this component. There is no skeleton — a placeholder for a progress display is a progress display. Before the first phase resolves, show the mark and the first label; never an empty rail.
EmptyFewer than three phases is not a timeline — use the Spinner and a label. More than four and the phases are too granular for a client: "Verify" is one phase, not four checks.
Content & laws

Say the phase, not the percentage.

Phase names are
one wordBuild · Deploy · Verify. Read · Extract · File. A phase whose name needs a clause is two phases, or a step the client should not have been shown.
Never a percentageA bar at 60% is a claim about the remaining 40% that nobody can honour. Named phases and an honest clock say the same thing without lying — and when a phase stalls, the label can say so.
The label is
present tense"Deploying to staging", not "Deploy" or "Deployed". The rail carries the noun; the line carries the verb, and the tense is what makes it feel live rather than logged.
One active nodeThe pulse means this is where the work is. Two pulses mean the caller is guessing. Phases are sequential by definition — parallel work is two jobs.
Essential motionThe spinner and the node pulse are marked data-motion="essential": under reduced motion they calm to 2.4s rather than stopping. Freezing them would delete the only signal that the job is alive, leaving a still picture indistinguishable from a stalled one.
It is not a logNo step counts, no timestamps per phase, no expandable detail. The chat's steps disclosure is where "what did it actually do" lives; this is a progress display, and adding history to it turns a calm moment into a console.
Do
Morph the label as phases pass. Count elapsed time up in tabular figures. Replace the whole thing with its outcome. Stop the rail where it failed and say which phase. Keep the phase count at three, four at most.
Don't
A percentage or an ETA. A generic spinner in place of the mark. Clickable phases. Two active nodes. Leaving a finished rail on screen. Per-phase timestamps. Announcing the rail as well as the label. Technical phase names borrowed from the pipeline.
Tokens used

Every value, named.

Colour--green-600 done + active nodes and passed connectors · --white node core, check and upcoming fill · --ink-200 upcoming ring and unpassed connectors · --red-700 the failed node · --ink-900 reached labels · --text-muted upcoming labels · --ink-600 the clock.
Motion--duration-spinner the mark's cycle · --duration-pulse the node pulse · --duration-soft node and label settle · --duration-slow connector tint, because a line filling wants to be seen · --ease-out throughout.
Geometry--radius-pill nodes and connectors. Phase column 78px, node 16px, core 5px, connector 62×2px at top 7px. Rhythm from the ladder: 8 node→label, 12 mark→label, 20 clock→rail.
