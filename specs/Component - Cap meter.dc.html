Design System
Component · Cap meter

What you bought, and what's left of it.

An allowance against its ceiling — twelve posts a month, a hundred and twenty buyers. It reports a fact the client agreed to, so it never scolds and never sells: reaching a cap you paid for is the plan working, not a problem.

Anatomy

A Chart Kit hbar that knows its ceiling.

GeometryBorrowed wholesale from chart/hbar: 110px label column, 8px rounded track, tabular value right. No new measurements — an allowance is a category comparison where one of the categories is the limit.
The track IS
the allowanceFull width means the cap, always. This is the one difference from an hbar: an hbar's track is scaled to the largest row, so a full bar just means "the biggest". Here a full bar means "all of it", and that must be true at a glance without reading the number.
Value8 of 12 — used, then the ceiling, in tabular figures. Never a bare number, never a percentage: the client bought twelve posts, not 67%.
The noteOne plain line beneath, and it only appears at or past the cap. Under the cap there is nothing to say, and a reassuring line where none is needed teaches the client to stop reading the ones that matter.
The tone ladder

Amber at the cap. Never red.

The obvious design turns the bar red when it fills. That would be wrong, and the reason is worth stating plainly: the client paid for exactly this much. Using all of it is the engagement working as sold. Red in this system means something failed and needs repair — spending it on a plan running to plan makes every real failure quieter.

Under--green-600. The default and the overwhelming majority of the time. No note, no chip, no attention drawn — a meter under its cap is a fact, not an event.
At the cap--amber-500 and one line saying what happens next ("Next month's twelve start on 1 Aug"). Amber in this system means this needs your attention, which is exactly right: nothing is broken, but the client should know the tap is closed until the period turns.
OverStill amber — and still not red. The overage is drawn as a hatched ink segment sitting past the amber fill, so the eye reads "beyond the line" rather than "danger". The note becomes the important part: how much over, and whether it is billed or absorbed. An overage is a commercial fact; the meter states it and the invoice settles it.

The eight states

A reading, not a control.

RestGreen fill, no note, value in --text-secondary. The state it is in almost always.
HoverWhere several meters sit as rows, the ROW tints --hover-on-white and the meter itself does not change. A meter that lights up on hover implies it can be clicked.
PressedDoes not apply. Nothing to press. Changing an allowance is a request through the chat, per the standing-job doctrine — never a stepper, never a plus button on the meter.
FocusDoes not apply to the meter. If the containing row is a link to the jobs behind the number, the ROW takes the focus ring; the meter is part of its content.
SelectedDoes not apply. A meter is never one of a set you choose between.
DisabledAn allowance not included in the plan renders as an --ink-100 track with no fill and "Not in your plan" where the value sits — greyed, but still listed, so the client can see what exists. Never hidden: a missing row cannot be asked about.
LoadingSkeleton the track and the value at their exact heights; keep the label real, since it is known before the count is. Never animate a fill from zero as a loading device — a growing bar reads as a real number climbing.
EmptyZero used takes an empty track and 0 of 12 — not an em-dash. Zero is a measurement here: the month has started and nothing has run yet, which is worth seeing. (Contrast with a table cell, where absence takes the dash.)
Content & laws

State the fact. Sell nothing.

Never an upsellNo "Upgrade to get more", no plan comparison, no chip at 80%. The console is the client's workspace, not a sales surface — and a meter that nags at eight of twelve makes the product feel metered rather than generous. If more is genuinely needed, the client asks and the Strategist answers.
Count the unit
the client bought"12 of 12 posts", "118 of 120 buyers". Never credits, tokens, units or API calls — an invented currency is how a client loses track of what they are paying for.
One period, namedEvery meter states its window once, in the note or the section head — never per row. A month and a quarter cannot share a group; if the plan mixes them, they are two groups.
Four meters, at mostDensity doctrine: one meter column per table, and an allowance group is a table. Beyond four the client is reading a bill, not a plan.
The bar and the
number must agreeThe fill is used / cap, clamped at 100%, and the number is never rounded. A bar that looks full beside "11 of 12" costs the client trust in every other number on the page.
No motion at restThe fill transitions on --duration-slow when the value genuinely changes, and otherwise holds still. Nothing pulses, nothing counts up on mount — an allowance is not activity.
Do
Say the unit the client recognises. Go amber only at the line, with one plain sentence about what happens next. Draw an overage as hatch beyond the fill. Keep the track's full width meaning the cap. Leave a meter under its cap completely silent.
Don't
Red at the cap. A percentage. A warning chip at 80%. An upgrade link. Credits or tokens. A plus or minus control. Hiding an allowance the plan excludes. Animating the fill on mount. More than four in one group.
Tokens used

Every value, named.

Colour--green-600 under-cap fill · --amber-500 at and over the cap · --ink-300 the overage hatch · --ink-100 the track · --status-caution-text the note · --text-secondary the value at rest, --ink-900 at 650 once the cap is met · --hover-on-white the containing row.
Motion--duration-slow + --ease-glide on width when the value changes · --duration-soft + --ease-out on the tone crossing. No keyframes, nothing essential — this component has no animation to preserve under reduced motion.
Geometry--radius-pill track and fill. Label column 110px, track 8px, value column 72px, grid gap 16 — all from chart/hbar. Note sits 8 below the meter (Pair), meters 12 apart in a group (Sibling).
