The refusals

What a partner cannot change.

Status coloursThe one that surprises people. Green means done, amber means it needs you, red means it failed — in every console, whatever the brand. A partner whose brand is red does not get red success states, and a partner whose brand is green does not get to make “held for you” green. Brand colour answers whose product is this; status colour answers what happened. Two different questions, and merging them costs the client the ability to scan.
The canvas and
the ink ladderThe console is a neutral cool-grey surface on purpose — it is not where personality lives. Tinting the canvas to a partner's brand makes every artifact inside it fight the background, and the artifacts hold the END CLIENT's brand, which is the one that has to win.
TypeThe system face at 13px. A partner's marketing typeface belongs on their marketing; a console runs on the reader's own system font because that is what stays legible at row density on every machine.
Geometry, rhythm
and motionRadii, the seven-rung ladder, the 180ms settle, the no-lift rule. These are not decoration — they are what make the product feel like one thing. A partner changing them is not rebranding, they are redesigning.
The mock interiorsA LinkedIn post looks like a LinkedIn post regardless of who resells the console. Mocks carry the destination's anatomy and the end client's palette \u2014 never the partner's.

Choosing the on-colour

Measured, never chosen.

Approve & publish {{ c.label }}
A bright signal takes black; a deep one takes white. Pick whichever clears 4.5:1 against the fill and state it in the brand file as --on-action-brand — never inherit it and never guess. Hivory's own green is the reason this rule exists: #00EE7F reads at 13.5:1 with black and 1.6:1 with white, so the instinct to put white on a coloured button would fail on our own brand.
Do
Keep the brand file to six declarations. Measure the on-colour. Supply the mark in the partner's own colour and a mono fallback. Test a partner theme against a client whose brand is a similar hue — that is where a white label breaks.
Don't
Brand-coloured status states. A tinted canvas. A partner typeface. Brand colour inside an artifact body. Two accents — a console has exactly one signal colour, and a partner with a primary and a secondary supplies the primary only.
