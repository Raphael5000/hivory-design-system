Design System
Foundation · Rhythm

Seven gaps. Each one means something.

Vertical space is how a screen says what belongs together. The 4px grid governs the inside of controls; it is the wrong instrument for rhythm, because at section scale it offers twenty indistinguishable options and the eye reads ratios, not increments.

Type held up
Nine sizes carry about 95% of all text, and they match the tokens. Where a token existed, we used it. The only drift is display sizes — 15.5, 22, 26, 38, 42, 54 — chosen ad hoc because the scale stops at 21px and hero moments had nothing to reach for.
Spacing did not
No token names a gap by its job, so every surface improvised — and the differences are sub-perceptual. Nobody can see 7 against 8, which is the tell: if a distinction cannot be seen, it is not carrying meaning, it is carrying entropy.
The ladder

Geometric, not linear.

Seven rungs, each roughly 1.6× the last, each with exactly one job. If you are reaching for an eighth, the answer is a different rung — not a new number. The seventh was found, not designed: every component page in the system was already using 84–100 for page-level breaks, and no rung could say it.

Why 1.6×
Two gaps read as different only when the larger is about half again the smaller. Below that the eye reads them as the same gap applied inconsistently — which is worse than either value, because it looks like a mistake rather than a decision.
The 4px grid still governs
Inside controls — button padding, field height, icon insets, row height. There the increments are small, bounded and load-bearing. The ladder governs the space between things; the grid governs the space within them.

The gap in the type scale

Display sizes, named at last.

The platform scale stops at 21px because the console has no hero moments. The proposal and marketing surfaces do, so they improvised — 22, 26, 34, 38, 42, 54, 66. Same ladder logic, four rungs, each about 1.35× the last.

Tracking tightens as size grows — that is not decoration. Default letter-spacing is set for reading sizes; at display sizes it reads as gaps between letters.
The laws

How to spend space.

Seven rungs, no others3 · 8 · 12 · 20 · 34 · 64 · 104. If a layout seems to want 26, it wants 20 or 34 — decide which side of the meaning it falls on. A seventh value is always a decision someone avoided making.
Space is the only
grouping deviceBefore reaching for a rule, a card, a wash or a border to show that things belong together, spend a rung. Most boxes in a first draft are a spacing problem wearing a container. The Glass Engine has one elevated card per screen precisely because space does the rest.
Gaps get bigger going upA label sits 3 from its value, the pair sits 8 from the next pair, the group sits 20 from the next group. Never let a child gap equal or exceed its parent gap — that is what makes a block read as ambiguous, and it is the single most common rhythm bug.
Section gaps are
asymmetricA heading belongs to what follows it, not what precedes it: 64 above, 12 below. Equal space above and below a heading orphans it between two blocks.
Eyebrow, heading, lede
are one unitThe eyebrow labels the heading and the lede explains it, so all three sit 12 apart regardless of the heading's size — 27px or 66px, the gap does not grow. Then 34 to the first block. Scaling that gap with the type is the reflex that makes big headings float.
The grid stays inside4px increments govern control interiors — padding, heights, insets, icon boxes. Never use the ladder for those, and never use the grid for vertical rhythm.
Drift rounds toward
contrast, not distanceWhen a stray colour collapses onto the ladder it rounds to the safer token, never the numerically nearest one. Learned the hard way on 31 July: #6E7072 sits between --ink-600 (Δ25) and --ink-500 (Δ16), the nearest was chosen, and twelve runs of 9pt secondary text on the signed contract fell from 4.9:1 to 3.3:1 — failing AA on the one deliverable that prints and cannot be zoomed. Same reasoning as the rungs: 26 resolves to 20 or 34 by meaning, not by distance. For colour, meaning is legibility.
Quiet text has a floorEvery text colour in the system clears 4.5:1 — including the quiet ones. --text-muted and --text-faint were re-graded on 31 July because they resolved to ink-500 (3.34:1) and ink-400 (2.63:1); they now carry their own values at 5.71 and 4.78. The ink ramp itself is unchanged — those steps are still right for borders, dots and meter tracks, which have no contrast requirement. Text ladder on white: 17.2 → 11.1 → 7.2 → 5.7 → 4.8.
Print rounds darker stillInk on paper has no backlight and no zoom, so every word in a document clears 4.5:1 — there is no exemption for labels, captions, footers or page numbers. Which means print hierarchy cannot be carried by greying out at all: it comes from size and weight, with tracking to quiet a label further. In the signed proposal all text sits at --ink-600 or darker, and an 8pt/400 label reads as subordinate to an 11pt/600 value without spending a single point of contrast. First draft of this law carved out "labels whose words are already known" — that was a rationalisation of nine runs measuring 2.63:1, and the measurement settled it.
Mocks are exemptAnything imitating another surface — every body in the Mock Kit — keeps the measurements of the thing it depicts. A 44px avatar and a 38px record row are load-bearing fidelity, not our rhythm. The ladder governs Hivory's own chrome and stops at the mock's edge.
Print has its own ladderDocuments are set in points against a fixed page, so they run a separate ladder — the px rungs do not convert. Set in the print spec, not here.
Measure caps holdReading prose ≤ 52ch on light surfaces, ≤ 46ch for lede paragraphs, ≤ 62ch in documents. Width is rhythm too — a line that runs past its cap makes every gap around it look wrong.
One family, tabular dataMono was retired on 27 July: --font-mono resolves to the system face. Machine facts are no longer marked by family — they are marked by font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums plus a quieter colour. Never reintroduce a second family to make data look like data.
Do
Pick the rung by asking what the gap separates, not by how it looks. Let a section breathe at 64 even when it feels generous — the density lives inside the blocks, not between them. Use asymmetric heading space. Delete a container every time a rung can do its job.
Don't
Nudge a value by 1–2px to fix an optical problem — the cause is almost always a wrong rung one level up. Equal gaps at two nesting levels. A new display size for one headline. Borders standing in for space. Sub-perceptual distinctions of any kind: if you can't see it, it isn't doing anything.
