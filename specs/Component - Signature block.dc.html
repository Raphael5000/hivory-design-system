Italic 300 at 27px on a 1px --ink-200 rule. It is not a script webfont and never should be: a fake handwriting face makes a typed name pretend to be ink, which is exactly the wrong claim on a document with legal weight. The italic says this stands for your signature without imitating one. The line keeps its 44px height when empty — a rule that grows as you type would move the button under your cursor.

Accessibility

Stricter than everything else.

Three tab stops in reading order: name, authorisation, sign. The CTA is a real button carrying disabled, so a screen reader announces it as unavailable rather than the client discovering it by pressing. The signature preview is aria-hidden — it repeats the field above it, and hearing your own name twice is noise. No motion on the signature itself: nothing fades or draws in as it renders, because animating a legal mark implies a ceremony that has not happened yet. Every word here clears 4.5:1, and the same block printed in the document holds --ink-600 or darker at every size.
Content rules

Say the consequence, then remove the fear.

The CTA names what is being agreed and what it costs — “Sign & accept — Full engine”, never “Submit”, “Confirm” or “Get started”. Directly beneath it, one line removing the fear the button creates: “Signing starts onboarding — no payment is taken today.” The authorisation checkbox is a positive first-person statement naming the company. The field label is “Full legal name”, not “Name” — the word legal is doing real work. After signing, the receipt states who signed, when, what and at what price, in one line, in past tense.
Do
Restate the deal directly above the signature, so nothing needs scrolling back. Keep the CTA disabled until both name and authorisation exist. Offer the printable document beside the button. Give a receipt the moment it is signed.
Don't
A handwriting webfont. Motion on the signature. A confirmation modal after it — the checkbox already was the confirmation, and a second gate reads as doubt. Terms hidden behind a link on this screen. Any word that hides the commitment.
